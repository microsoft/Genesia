## Summary

Design A helps participants find pending reviews, recover the basis for work
and distinguish content acceptance from permission to send. Prioritize
supporting-text readability and authorization terminology while retaining the
parallel layout and independent decision boundaries. Whether specific contexts
justify reliance still requires validation of sources, access and responsibility.

- Task location is clear, but some supporting text is small/pale and burdens reading.
- The distinction between content acceptance and sending permission is understood,
  while some technical terms still need explanation.
- Visible information offers a starting point for judgment, not a substitute for
  content, access-scope and responsibility checks.
- Critical usage conditions for a minority limit the applicability of overall
  positive feedback.
- Synthetic Persona feedback, not research with real participants or a population estimate.

## Findings

<a id="finding-f1"></a>

### Retain parallel orientation; improve supporting text

All 20 answers affirm the orientation value of the task list, selected material
and decision panel appearing together. Eight note supporting text that is small,
pale or harder to read; 2 find Workstream easier to read than message-permission
or technical details. Participants want current task context while checking
decision information, so layout strengths and text-reading burdens need separate
treatment. Some approve of the organization of authorization information:
reading difficulty is not overall layout failure.

Evidence contributors: 20 from cited supporting answers; 0 from cited counter answers. These sets may overlap and are not votes on every claim.

- All 20 report or affirm parallel orientation: the spatial relationship among task list, selected material and decision panel helps retain task context and locate/compare decision information. (supporting answers: 20; counter answers: 0)
- Eight note small, pale or more demanding supporting text; 2 find Workstream easier than message-permission/technical details. Other concerns about mobile, magnification and screen-reader adaptation remain unverified and do not establish failures. (supporting answers: 9; counter answers: 4)
- To validate: Text size/contrast were not measured. Mobile, magnification and screen-reader concerns are not observed failures of those paths.

<a id="finding-f2"></a>

### Authorization terminology adds comprehension effort

Participants find “message.publish” and “one-time action intent” harder than
everyday permission explanations. One participant in the comprehension question
understands that accepting content does not authorize sending and knows how to
continue through “Review result,” yet still needs terminology explained.
Explicit explanations and independent acceptance records help some understand
that sending still requires permission. Improve local wording rather than
merge the decision boundaries again.

Evidence contributors: 9 from cited supporting answers; 6 from cited counter answers. These sets may overlap and are not votes on every claim.

<details><summary>View evidence details</summary>

- One understands content acceptance versus sending permission and continuation through “Review result,” but reports terms such as “message.publish” need explanation. (supporting answers: 1; counter answers: 0)
- Participants find “message.publish” and “one-time action intent” harder than everyday permission explanations. Small/pale supporting text also adds effort. Explicit explanations and separate content-acceptance records help some understand that sending needs additional permission. (supporting answers: 9; counter answers: 6)
- To validate: No evidence shows that terminology caused incorrect authorization or actual action failure.

<a id="finding-f3"></a>

### Permission scope does not replace credibility checks

Some accept the review logic but still want the complete message, research
support, Agent/shared-device access scope, destination recipients, and who sets
permissions or owns the handover. They intend to verify content, access and
responsibility, not merely understand a permission label. Citations, mode labels
and one-time permission information in the supplied materials do not answer
these questions. That does not establish missing product capabilities or
failed controls.

Evidence contributors: 14 from cited supporting answers; 0 from cited counter answers. These sets may overlap and are not votes on every claim.

<details><summary>View evidence details</summary>

- Some accept the review logic while needing the complete message, source quality, business permissions or shared-device safeguards for concrete judgment. These are unresolved conditions, not proof of missing content or failed controls in Design A. (supporting answers: 9; counter answers: 0)
- Beyond content, some want to know which materials Design A's Agent/shared devices access, who proposed the message/set permission, who receives a destination, and who owns the next handover step. Generic version/one-time-permission information cannot replace these concerns. (supporting answers: 9; counter answers: 0)
- Participants try to verify research support, message accuracy, Agent-accessible information, and who sets destination/permissions. Visible citations/mode labels do not resolve this. Another understands notes are not sent to an Agent but remains unsure who can read them on shared devices. (supporting answers: 8; counter answers: 0)
- To validate: Real access, permission enforcement, sending and handover outcomes were not verified.

<a id="finding-f4"></a>

### Content acceptance and external sending permission are distinct

All 20 understand Accept v3 as accepting the current document/artifact without
publishing; 19 also understand Authorize once as permission only for a specific
message version and destination, not continuing authorization. Version,
destination and one-time permission clarify the object of approval. Some find
this separation more reassuring or clearer about sharing responsibility.

Evidence contributors: 20 from cited supporting answers; 0 from cited counter answers. These sets may overlap and are not votes on every claim.

<details><summary>View evidence details</summary>

- All 20 understand Design A's Accept v3 as accepting the current document/artifact without publishing; 19 also understand Authorize once as permission for the specified message version/destination only, not a continuing grant. Some explicitly find the separation reassuring or clearer about sharing responsibility. (supporting answers: 20; counter answers: 0)
- Participants say that separating acceptance from sending authorization while displaying exact version, destination and one-time permission clarifies what they approve and prevents treating approval of wording as permission to share. (supporting answers: 19; counter answers: 0)
- To validate: No contrary understanding was reported, but not mentioning a control is not opposition. Answers do not establish that permission limits were enforced.

### Pending-work entry and history help recover the basis for decisions

Participants want to identify work needing their judgment before recovering
context. “Needs you” provides a starting point; Workstream explains progress,
Context provides goals/requirements, and Result points to content for inspection.
Linked feedback/revisions focus checks; research scope and time estimates help
weigh coverage. This gives direction to Agent supervision, not completed content
verification or actual decisions.

Evidence contributors: 20 from cited supporting answers; 0 from cited counter answers. These sets may overlap and are not votes on every claim.

<details><summary>View evidence details</summary>

- Participants want to identify pending work, understand prepared content or follow handovers. Design A's document/research/message examples are useful, especially for distinguishing content acceptance from sending authorization. This is process understanding, not actual approval/delivery success. (supporting answers: 20; counter answers: 0)
- Participants describe Design A as supervising Agent work and making task-level decisions. Statuses such as “Needs you” provide a start, Workstream explains how a task reached its current stage, Context supplies goals/requirements, and Result/“Review result” point to content for inspection. (supporting answers: 20; counter answers: 0)

Understanding the concept and readiness to rely on it are different thresholds;
overall positive feedback does not cancel critical minority conditions.

## Limitations

This report uses feedback from 20 synthetic participants on static captures,
without statistical significance or population inference. No acceptance,
revision, authorization, delivery or completion outcome was supplied. Content
outside the viewport, free scrolling, arbitrary input and persistence were not
tested. Tasks, identities and artifacts are synthetic demo content. Recorded
clicks are not live operation or complete task validation.
